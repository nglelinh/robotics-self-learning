Estimated time: **75–90 minutes**, including one at-rest log and one 90-degree tilt. No motors.

## Learning objectives

You will state what an accelerometer actually reports (specific force, not an angle) and check that a resting board has magnitude about 1 g. You will treat a gyroscope as a rate in degrees per second, and you will compute how many degrees a constant bias becomes after a minute of integration. You will run one step of a complementary filter with the numbers in this lesson, and you will convert an MPU-6050 raw count with the datasheet scale instead of printing the integer as degrees. You will record the at-rest sign of each axis before any tilt formula is allowed near your notes.

## Prerequisites

Lesson 04-01 is the I2C scan: you should already be able to see `0x68` or `0x69` before you ask a library for acceleration. Lesson 04-02 is how you recognized a GY-521 and did not confuse it with a rangefinder. Chapter 03 is enough firmware to print three numbers on a timer. Trigonometry for `atan2` is assumed. ROS is not.

## Why this matters for Capstone A and the ROS path

Capstone A can teleop in Chapter 07 with no IMU. The robot’s job there is to start on a command and stop on a timeout or a range reading. A gyro that has drifted is not part of that safety story, and bolting one on does not make the timeout optional.

`sensor_msgs/Imu` comes after that stop already works. The message carries angular velocity in rad/s, linear acceleration in m/s², and an orientation quaternion you should leave unset (covariance −1 on the diagonal) until a filter you trust has earned it. The complementary filter in this lesson is a pitch estimate for your notebook, not that quaternion. Madgwick and Kalman wait until the raw axes, the bias, and the scale are boringly correct. A ROS plot of “degrees” that are still LSB counts is a firmware bug with a nicer font.

## Specific force is not a tilt angle

An accelerometer measures specific force: acceleration relative to free fall. On the table the board is not accelerating in the lab, but the table pushes up on it, so the reading is about 1 g along the axis that points up. The magnitude

$$
\sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2 + a_z^2} \approx 1~\mathrm{g}
$$

is the check that the scale is sane. It is not yet a pitch angle. Pitch appears only after you assume the long-term acceleration is gravity and you know which way the chip thinks is up.

State this frame out loud and then measure it: $$x$$ forward, $$y$$ left, $$z$$ up. If the board is flat and $$a_z$$ reads $$+g$$, a nose-up pitch is

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{acc}} \approx \operatorname{atan2}(-a_x, a_z).
$$

Nose up rotates the forward axis toward the sky, the upward push from the table gains a component along $$-x$$, and $$-a_x$$ becomes positive. Students who paste the formula before they have written the at-rest signs will “calibrate” a level robot to a large angle. If your flat board reads $$a_z \approx -g$$, the chip or the breakout is upside down relative to this sentence, and the formula is not yours yet. Record the sign, then flip the axis in the notes, then filter.

## A gyro is a rate

The gyroscope reports angular rate, in deg/s or rad/s, about those same axes. It does not report an angle. An angle is something you build by summing rate times time, and every constant error in the rate is a ramp in the angle. A bias of $$0.5~\mathrm{deg/s}$$ left in the sum for 60 s becomes

$$
0.5 \times 60 = 30~\mathrm{deg}
$$

of fake rotation while the robot never yawed. That is why an at-rest log of the gyro, in deg/s after the scale, is part of the lab. Subtract that bias before you integrate. It will still wander with temperature; subtracting the first reading only removes the bias you had at that minute.

## One complementary step

Over a short time the integrated gyro is the better angle. Over a long time the accelerometer’s tilt is the better angle, because the bias ramp would otherwise win. A complementary filter blends them:

$$
\theta \leftarrow \alpha\,(\theta + \omega \Delta t) + (1 - \alpha)\,\theta_{\mathrm{acc}}.
$$

With $$\alpha = 0.98$$ and $$\Delta t = 0.01~\mathrm{s}$$, the accelerometer is allowed a time constant on the order of $$\Delta t / (1 - \alpha) = 0.5~\mathrm{s}$$. Fast motion stays with the gyro. The steady tilt leaks in from $$\theta_{\mathrm{acc}}$$.

Work one step by hand. Previous $$\theta = 10~\mathrm{deg}$$, gyro $$\omega = 5~\mathrm{deg/s}$$, accelerometer tilt $$\theta_{\mathrm{acc}} = 2~\mathrm{deg}$$:

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{gyro}} = 10 + 5 \times 0.01 = 10.05~\mathrm{deg},
$$

$$
\theta = 0.98 \times 10.05 + 0.02 \times 2 = 9.849 + 0.04 = 9.889~\mathrm{deg}.
$$

The estimate moved a little toward the gyro’s new angle and a little toward 2 degrees. It did not jump to 2 degrees, and it did not ignore the accelerometer. Units have to match: if $$\omega$$ is in deg/s, $$\theta$$ is in degrees. Mixing radians on one side is a silent factor of $$\pi/180$$.

This is the whole fusion story for the course until much later. Do not import a nine-axis library to avoid writing these three lines.

## MPU-6050 counts are not degrees

The usual breakout is a GY-521, I2C address `0x68` with AD0 low and `0x69` with AD0 high. Full-scale ranges are $$\pm 2, 4, 8, 16~\mathrm{g}$$ and $$\pm 250$$ to $$2000~\mathrm{deg/s}$$. The chip returns 16-bit integers. At $$\pm 2~\mathrm{g}$$ the accelerometer scale is $$16384~\mathrm{LSB/g}$$. A resting $$a_z$$ near $$+16384$$ is $$+1~\mathrm{g}$$, not “16384 degrees.” At $$\pm 250~\mathrm{deg/s}$$ the gyro scale is $$131~\mathrm{LSB/(deg/s)}$$. A raw gyro reading of 70 is

$$
\omega = \frac{70}{131} \approx 0.53~\mathrm{deg/s},
$$

and an hour of trusting the raw 70 as deg/s would be fiction. Set the range in the configuration registers to the range whose sensitivity you divide by. A library that hides the divide is fine only after you have printed both the raw integer and the scaled value once and seen the factor with your own eyes.

## Lab: rest, then a right angle

### Safety

USB power only. Do not spin a wheel in this lab. The module stays on the bench. If the chip becomes hot, AD0, VCC, and GND are probably not what you think; unplug before you reflow anything with your fingers.

### BOM

| Item | Role |
|------|------|
| GY-521 or other MPU-6050 breakout | The IMU |
| ESP32 or Pico | I2C master |
| Breadboard, short wires | SDA, SCL, 3.3 V, GND |
| A book or the bench edge | A repeatable 90-degree tilt |
| `lab-notes.md` | Signs, 1 g check, bias in deg/s |

### Steps

1. Scan I2C. You want `0x68` or `0x69`, and you want it stable across two scans. No address, no scaling.
2. Configure $$\pm 2~\mathrm{g}$$ and $$\pm 250~\mathrm{deg/s}$$, or read back whatever the library set, and write the LSB-per-unit numbers next to it.
3. Leave the board flat and still for a few seconds. Log $$a_x, a_y, a_z$$ in g and the three gyro axes in deg/s. Magnitude should sit near 1 g. Gyro channels should sit near 0 and not on 0. The average of a second of gyro data is your bias.
4. Tilt the board 90 degrees so the axis you called forward points up. The ~1 g reading should leave $$z$$ and appear on $$x$$ (or on $$-x$$, if your forward axis pointed down). Write which axis swapped. If none swap, you are tilting the wrong way or scaling a register you never configured.
5. If the module has not arrived, do not invent a table. Write a dated waiver, compute the complementary step to 9.889 degrees, and compute the 30 degree drift from 0.5 deg/s. The checklist accepts that waiver. It does not accept a blank line.

### Expected results

A sentence of the form “flat, $$a_z = +0.98~\mathrm{g}$$, $$|a| = 1.02~\mathrm{g}$$, gyro bias $$(0.4, -0.2, 0.6)~\mathrm{deg/s}$$” using your numbers, plus “90-degree tilt moved the 1 g reading from $$z$$ onto $$x$$.” Raw LSB values may sit beside the scaled ones. They may not replace them.

### Faults

| What you see | What it usually is |
|--------------|--------------------|
| Magnitude 2 g or 0.5 g at rest | Wrong full-scale, so the wrong sensitivity |
| Gyro “bias” of tens of deg/s | You forgot to divide by 131, or the board is still in your hand |
| Pitch formula says 180 degrees on a level desk | $$a_z$$ is negative and you applied $$\operatorname{atan2}(-a_x, a_z)$$ anyway |
| Scan shows nothing | 5 V into a 3.3 V module, SDA/SCL swapped, or no common ground |
| Two devices and neither ACKs | A second MPU at the same address; move AD0 |

## Exercises

1. Run the complementary step: $$\alpha = 0.98$$, $$\Delta t = 0.01~\mathrm{s}$$, $$\theta = 10~\mathrm{deg}$$, $$\omega = 5~\mathrm{deg/s}$$, $$\theta_{\mathrm{acc}} = 2~\mathrm{deg}$$. What is the next $$\theta$$?
2. A gyro bias of $$0.5~\mathrm{deg/s}$$ is integrated for 60 s. How many degrees of drift is that? How many degrees is a bias of $$0.8~\mathrm{deg/s}$$ integrated for 2 minutes?
3. Full scale is $$\pm 2~\mathrm{g}$$ ($$16384~\mathrm{LSB/g}$$) and $$\pm 250~\mathrm{deg/s}$$ ($$131~\mathrm{LSB/(deg/s)}$$). Raw $$a_z = 16384$$ and raw $$\omega_z = 70$$. Give both in physical units. Why is printing 16384 as degrees wrong twice?
4. The flat board reads $$a_z = +1~\mathrm{g}$$. After a nose-up tilt, $$a_x = -0.50~\mathrm{g}$$ and $$a_z = 0.866~\mathrm{g}$$. Estimate pitch with $$\operatorname{atan2}(-a_x, a_z)$$.
5. The same board, mounted upside down, reads $$a_z = -1~\mathrm{g}$$ and $$a_x = 0$$ while it is level. What does the unmodified formula return, and what do you write in the notes before you filter?

### Answer guidance

1. $$\theta_{\mathrm{gyro}} = 10.05$$, then $$0.98 \times 10.05 + 0.02 \times 2 = 9.889~\mathrm{deg}$$. 2. $$30~\mathrm{deg}$$, and $$0.8 \times 120 = 96~\mathrm{deg}$$. 3. $$+1.00~\mathrm{g}$$ and $$70/131 \approx 0.53~\mathrm{deg/s}$$. The integer is LSB, not degrees, and it is an acceleration channel besides. 4. $$\operatorname{atan2}(0.50, 0.866) = 30^\circ$$. 5. $$\operatorname{atan2}(0, -1) = 180^\circ$$ on a level board. Write the measured at-rest signs and change the axis map before using the formula.
