Estimated time: **~75 minutes**.

## Learning objectives

You will replace `delay()` in the motor loop with a `millis()` deadline, log the achieved period for a few hundred ticks, and decide from the 95th percentile whether 50 Hz is honest. You will move `Serial.print`, Wi-Fi, and JSON work off the fast path, and you will state what a late PID sample does to $$K_d$$. The log from this lesson is part of the Capstone’s acceptance notes before any ROS 2 bridge is trusted.

## Prerequisites

Lessons 08-01 through 08-04 gave you a frame, an optional MQTT client, a PID update, and a kinematics function. You can read `millis()` on an ESP32 or Pico and you know that `delay(20)` waits twenty milliseconds *after* the work, not *instead of* a variable amount of work. A laptop serial monitor is enough. Motors may stay disconnected; the timing log does not need them, and it is safer that way until the numbers look calm.

## Why this sits on the path

The derivative term divides by $$\Delta t$$. If you claim $$\Delta t = 0.02$$ while the real gap was 0.08 because MQTT reconnected, $$K_d$$ is four times too strong for that sample and the wheel chatters. Odometry that multiplies speed by “20 ms” while the loop actually took 35 ms walks the map. ROS 2 will not repair a lie about time. `use_sim_time` in Chapter 10 is the simulator’s version of the same honesty: one clock, and everybody uses it. A policy that consumes actions at 10 Hz still sits on a current loop that must be boring.

## Concepts

A control period is the time from one *sample* to the next sample, not the time you slept. If reading encoders takes 1 ms, PID takes 0.2 ms, and `delay(20)` runs after that, the period is about 21.2 ms and it grows whenever you print a paragraph. Jitter is the spread of those periods. A loop that is usually 20 ms and sometimes 90 ms has a pretty average and a bad tail. The tail is what saturates D and drops USB bytes.

The deadline pattern:

```text
next = millis() + PERIOD_MS
every iteration:
    now = millis()
    if (int32_t)(now - next) < 0: return   # not due yet; do housekeeping
    dt_ms = now - prev
    prev = now
    next += PERIOD_MS
    if dt_ms > 5 * PERIOD_MS: dt_ms = PERIOD_MS  # after a stall, do not take a huge D step
    control_step(dt_ms / 1000.0)
```

Use a signed subtraction so `millis()` rollover after ~49 days still works. Catching up with `next += PERIOD_MS` instead of `next = now + PERIOD_MS` keeps the average rate honest when you are only slightly late. If you are very late, skip ahead (`next = now + PERIOD_MS`) so you do not fire ten PID updates back to back.

What is allowed inside the 20 ms tick: read encoders, compute kinematics if a new command arrived, run PID, write PWM, update the watchdog. What is not: `Serial.print` of five lines, `client.connect()`, parsing a large JSON document, `delay` of any kind, or waiting on Wi-Fi. Telemetry goes out every 5th or 10th tick, one short line. MQTT `client.loop()` may run on the ticks between deadlines, and a reconnect may run at 1 Hz from a separate counter. If reconnect blocks, the watchdog in lesson 08-02 still zeros the motors; the timing log tells you that you paid for that safety with a gap.

A practical budget for this robot is 50 Hz (20 ms) for the motor loop and 10 Hz for telemetry. 100 Hz is fine if the log says the 95th percentile period is under about 12 ms for a 10 ms target. Faster than you can measure is not a badge of honor.

The number to write down is not the mean alone. Sort the periods and report p50 and p95, or at least the maximum. A p95 inside $$0.8$$ to $$1.2$$ times the target is a pass for this course. A single 200 ms gap while “the mean is 21 ms” is a fail until you know which call caused it.

## Worked example

Forty periods, target 20 ms. Suppose thirty-eight of them are 20 or 21, one is 28, and one is 74 because a debug print dumped a buffer. The mean is about 22.5 ms, which looks acceptable, and p95 is 28 ms, which also looks acceptable if you compute the percentile without the story. The maximum is 74 ms. That one sample fed $$K_d$$ a $$\Delta t$$ almost four times the design value if you used the real gap, or it fed a stale derivative if you pretended $$\Delta t$$ was 0.02. The lab asks you to print the max and to comment the line that caused it. “Mostly 20 ms” is not the report.

A tiny host-side reducer:

```python
import statistics as stats
periods = [20, 21, 20, 19, 22, 20, 74, 21, 20, 28]
print("p50", stats.median(periods), "max", max(periods))
# p95 for a short list: sort and index
s = sorted(periods)
print("p95~", s[max(0, int(0.95 * len(s)) - 1)])
```

On the board, you do not need a full histogram. Keep a running max and a count of ticks that exceeded `PERIOD_MS + 4`.

## Lab

Motors unplugged for the first log. Firmware prints one CSV line every tick for 5 seconds, then stops printing. Printing every tick *is* the disturbance you are measuring, so also take a second log that prints only a summary.

```text
dt_ms
20
21
20
...
```

Summary line after 250 ticks:

```text
n=250 p50=20 p95=22 max=27 over=3
```

Then enable the real workload: MQTT client or a burst of `Serial.print` inside the tick. Capture the summary again.

**Expected**

| Build | p95 | max | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline loop, summary only | ≤ 24 ms | ≤ 30 ms | pass for 50 Hz |
| `delay(20)` plus a 40-character print every tick | often > 30 ms | prints dominate | fail, move the print |
| MQTT reconnect inside the tick | one sample of hundreds of ms | the reconnect | fail, move reconnect off the tick |

**Failure modes**

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Every period is 0 or 1 | You forgot to wait for the deadline; the loop is spinning |
| Periods climb forever: 20, 40, 60 | `next` is not updated, so one late tick stays late in the bookkeeping, or you stack `delay` |
| max is huge only when the cable is loose | USB serial blocking on a full buffer. Shorten the print |
| PID hisses only when telemetry is on | Logging is inside the tick. The gains were tuned against a lie |
| Pico and ESP32 disagree | `millis()` on both is fine; a `time.sleep` in MicroPython is the same bug as `delay` |

MicroPython note: `time.ticks_ms()` and `time.ticks_diff()` are the rollover-safe pair. `time.sleep_ms(20)` after the work recreates the jitter figure.

## Exercises

1. A tick’s real $$\Delta t$$ is 0.08 s and the code still divides the derivative by 0.02. By what factor is that D term wrong? Guidance: four times too large, because the true rate of change is smaller than the one you assumed.
2. Why is the maximum a better alarm than the mean? Guidance: the mean hides one blocking call. The wheel feels the blocking call.
3. Rewrite the pseudo-code so a tick that is 200 ms late does not immediately run five makeup PID steps. Guidance: if the lateness exceeds one period, set `next = now + PERIOD_MS` and clamp `dt`.
4. Where do you put `mosquitto` traffic relative to the deadline? Guidance: not inside the due tick. Between deadlines, and never blocking past the next sample without the watchdog firing.
5. What will you paste into the Chapter 10 bridge issue if p95 was 45 ms on a 20 ms target? Guidance: “do not tune Nav2 until the base loop’s p95 is inside 20 percent of its period.” That is a real gate, not a slogan.
