Estimated time: **~80 minutes**.

## Learning objectives

You will compare one Capstone command in Gazebo Harmonic with the same command on the bench, using the same units, the same REP-105 frame names, and the same wheel sign. You will keep a single `/clock` in simulation and refuse `use_sim_time` on the real robot, where the clock is the wall clock. You will measure how old a `cmd_vel` stamp is, connect that age to the 300 ms watchdog, and add the extra delay Wi-Fi or MQTT can insert. You will explain why an E-stop has to open the motor power path in hardware, not only publish a topic. You will fill three checklist rows with pass or fail and attach evidence: a command transcript or a measurement, not a feeling.

## Prerequisites

Lesson 10-02’s spawned model rolls forward at `linear.x` 0.2 in the Harmonic world. Lesson 10-04’s bridge turns that same Twist into `V,40,40` and then `V,0,0` when the publisher stops. Lesson 09-05’s frame names, `odom` and `base_link` at minimum, are what both sides must use. The robot pack, the common ground, and the wheels-up habit from Chapter 07 still apply the moment a real driver is connected. You can do the writing part of the lab from logs you already captured.

## Why this sits on the path

Simulation let you see `/cmd_vel` without burning a motor. The floor is where that topic has to mean the same thing. Nav2 has no separate mode that fixes units, frame names, or a backwards wheel. A policy trained later in Harmonic transfers only as far as metres, frames, sign, and a stop still hold when the network stalls. Walk this gate before autonomy on the real base, and again when you change a wheel, a sensor mount, or the bridge scale. A poster does not catch a second odometry publisher. Evidence does.

## Concepts

Distances in this course are metres. Speeds on `/cmd_vel` are metres per second and radians per second. The URDF, the diff-drive plugin, and the bridge’s track $$L = 0.16$$ are all in metres. The wire integer is not a third unit system. It exists only after lesson 10-04’s scale, where 0.5 m/s of tread is integer 100. A world left in millimetres, or a bridge that treats `linear.x` as “about 20,” makes the same published number mean two different motions.

[REP-105](https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0105.html) names `map`, `odom`, `base_link`, and the sensor frames. The plugin, `robot_state_publisher`, and any odometry from the bridge have to use those strings identically. A URDF link `laser` with a scan `frame_id` of `laser_link` leaves TF with no edge, so the cloud sits at the origin and the costmap looks like a tuning problem. REP-103 rides along: $$+x$$ forward, $$+y$$ left, $$+z$$ up, positive yaw to the left.

In the Harmonic world, `linear.x = 0.2` must translate the model along its $$+x$$, both wheels rolling forward. On the bench the same Twist must roll both real wheels forward. At the course scale the payload is `V,40,40`. A motor with swapped leads rolls backward while the integer is positive. Fix the leads or the sign in one place, then repeat the Twist on both sides. Negating $$\omega$$ only inside Nav2 hides the bug until the next teleop node.

Time is the contract people copy blindly from a sim launch file. In Harmonic there is one clock, bridged one way:

```bash
ros2 run ros_gz_bridge parameter_bridge /clock@rosgraph_msgs/msg/Clock[gz.msgs.Clock
```

Nodes that share that time set `use_sim_time` true. Confirm a single publisher with `ros2 topic info /clock`. On the real robot you do not bridge a clock and you do not set `use_sim_time` true. A node left in sim time waits for `/clock`, its timers sit still, and the motors keep the last command. The cause is a copied launch argument.

Latency is the part simulation will not volunteer. Stamp age is

$$
\mathrm{age} = t_{\mathrm{now}} - t_{\mathrm{stamp}}
$$

with both times from the same clock. On a USB dry run the age is a few milliseconds. Chapter 08’s Wi-Fi and MQTT path can add tens or hundreds of milliseconds, and a stalled router can add seconds. The bridge already turns a silent Twist into `V,0,0` after 0.3 s, and the firmware does it again if frames stop. An age past 0.3 s is already older than that timeout. Do not raise the watchdog to two seconds so the robot keeps sprinting after the joystick dies.

The E-stop is not that timeout. A watchdog dies with the process, which is the failure you are covering. The emergency stop is a switch in the motor supply that cuts driver power when you hit it. A ROS topic or a GPIO is a polite extra. The switch still works when the ESP32 has locked up. Mount it where a hand reaches it without crossing the wheels.

Power wiring is part of the same gate. Common ground between the logic and the motor driver, a fuse on the battery feed, and the first motion test with the wheels off the table. USB never feeds the motors. Those rules are older than ROS; sim-to-real is where people drop them because the simulator has no pack to catch fire.

The course rangefinder, the [DFRobot TF-Luna](https://hshop.vn/cam-bien-khoang-cach-dfrobot-tf-luna-tof-micro-single-point-ranging-lidar), returns one range along its axis. It is not a 360° SLAM lidar. A Gazebo GPU lidar with a full ring is a different instrument. You may simulate a ring to learn Nav2, and you must not expect the TF-Luna to reproduce that ring. An IMU such as the [GY-521](https://hshop.vn/cam-bien-6-dof-bac-tu-do-gy-521-mpu6050) needs the same rule: one `frame_id`, the one in the URDF.

Harmonic’s default contact is not your carpet, and the plugin is not the PID from lesson 08-03. Copying a simulated wheel-PID onto the floor oscillates or crawls. Retune on the real mechanism, wheels up first, then on the floor. The matching software failure is two odometry publishers, both broadcasting `odom` to `base_link`. `ros2 topic echo /odom` can look fine while TF flips. `ros2 topic info /tf -v` shows both broadcasters.

Put the comparisons in one place and then test rows, not the whole table in the abstract.

| | Simulation (Harmonic) | Real Capstone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linear unit on `/cmd_vel` | m/s | m/s, then one scale to −100…100 on the wire |
| Angular unit | rad/s about $$+z$$ | the same `angular.z`, same sign |
| Frames | REP-105 names from the URDF | the same strings in the bridge and any scan or IMU |
| Wheel sign | $$+x$$ moves the model forward | the same Twist rolls both real wheels forward |
| Clock | one `/clock`, Gazebo to ROS, `use_sim_time` true | wall time, `use_sim_time` false, no fake `/clock` |
| Command age | usually a few milliseconds | measure it; MQTT or Wi-Fi from Chapter 08 adds more |
| Stop when the link dies | pause the world, or publish zeros | firmware watchdog at 300 ms, plus a hardware E-stop |
| First motion | spawn, then a short Twist | wheels up, fused pack, common ground, then the same Twist |
| Ground contact | collision meshes on the plane | carpet and casters, which are not the sim friction |
| Odometry | one publisher of `odom` → `base_link` | one publisher; a second one fights TF |

## Worked example

Acceptance for the straight command, written before you touch the hardware.

In simulation, with the lesson 10-02 stack up:

```bash
ros2 topic pub --times 20 /cmd_vel geometry_msgs/msg/Twist \
  "{linear: {x: 0.2, y: 0.0, z: 0.0}, angular: {x: 0.0, y: 0.0, z: 0.0}}"
```

Pass: the model in the Gazebo window translates forward and both wheels roll. Fail: it yaws, it drives backward, or only RViz moves.

On the bench, wheels up, bridge on the real port, same Twist for a short burst and then silence. Pass: the parser or the bridge log shows `V,40,40`, both wheels turn forward for less than a second, and they stop when the publisher stops, with `V,0,0` on the wire. Fail: one wheel reverses, they keep turning after silence, or the integers are not 40.

A third row you can close from the computer alone is the clock. In sim:

```bash
ros2 topic info /clock
```

Pass: one publisher, the bridge, and `use_sim_time` is true only on the nodes that are supposed to follow Gazebo. On the robot launch, pass is the absence of that parameter set to true. Search the launch files for `use_sim_time` before you call the row done. A copied sim launch fails this row even when the wheels are not connected.

Stamp age, if you log it in the bridge callback, uses the formula above. A USB loopback at a few milliseconds passes a “under 50 ms” note. A number near 0.3 s is a warning, not a pass, even if the wheels still moved.

## Lab

1. Open `lab-notes.md` and copy the table. You will not tick every row tonight. Choose three: wheel sign, clock, and one of command-age, E-stop, or single odometry publisher.
2. Wheel sign: run the 0.2 m/s burst in Harmonic and write pass or fail with what you saw in the world. On the bench, wheels up, run the same burst through the bridge. Record the parser line and which way each wheel turned. Stop as soon as the direction is obvious.
3. Clock: record `ros2 topic info /clock` from the sim session, and record the `use_sim_time` lines you actually launch on the robot. A robot command with that parameter true is a fail, even if you did not plug the motors in.
4. Third row, your choice. For age, print one measured $$t_{\mathrm{now}} - t_{\mathrm{stamp}}$$. For the E-stop, either find the switch in the motor supply or write fail and the sentence “stop is only a topic.” For odometry, run `ros2 topic info /tf -v` and name every node that publishes `odom` → `base_link`.
5. Write one action for any failed row. “Retune later” is not an action. “Swap the left motor leads, then repeat the 0.2 m/s burst” is an action.

**Expected**

Three rows, each with pass or fail and a piece of evidence. A fully passing wheel-sign pair looks like this:

```text
sim: linear.x 0.2, model moved forward in Gazebo, both wheels rolled
bench: parser OK V,40,40, both wheels forward, then OK V,0,0 within 1 s of silence
clock: sim has one /clock publisher; robot launch does not set use_sim_time true
```

Your third row may legitimately fail. A fail with evidence passes the lab. A pass with no transcript does not.

**Failure modes**

| What you see | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Sim drives well, floor oscillates or crawls | Simulated friction or plugin gains were copied onto carpet. Retune lesson 08-03 on the real wheels |
| Cloud or ranger sits at the origin in RViz | `frame_id` is `laser_link` in the message and `laser` in the URDF, or the reverse |
| Robot jumps in RViz, echo of `/odom` looks smooth | Two publishers on `odom` → `base_link`. `ros2 topic info /tf -v` shows both |
| Nodes start, timers never fire, wheels keep the last PWM | `use_sim_time` true on the robot, and nothing publishes `/clock` |
| Joystick released, robot runs for seconds | Watchdog longer than 300 ms, or Wi-Fi retained a stale command and nothing zeros it |
| E-stop “works” in `ros2 topic echo` and does nothing when the MCU locks | The stop is only a subscription. The supply is still connected |

## Exercises

1. Simulation nodes use `use_sim_time` true. You copy the launch to the robot and forget to change it. What stops updating, and which topic is missing? Guidance: timers and TF waits stall because the robot publishes no `/clock`, while the last motor command can remain.
2. The same Twist drives forward in Gazebo and spins the left wheel backward on the bench. Which table row failed, and what do you change first? Guidance: the wheel-sign row; swap that motor’s leads or the sign in one bridge place, then repeat the 0.2 m/s burst on both sides.
3. `ros2 topic echo /odom` looks smooth, yet the model twitches in RViz. Which command shows the second broadcaster that the echo hides? Guidance: `ros2 topic info /tf -v`, looking for two nodes on the `odom` to `base_link` edge.
4. A Wi-Fi path delivers a Twist whose stamp is 0.45 s behind `t_now`. Write the age and say whether the watchdog should still be tracking that command. Guidance: age is $$t_{\mathrm{now}} - t_{\mathrm{stamp}} = 0.45$$ s, which is already past the 0.3 s limit, so the bridge should be sending zeros.
5. Why is a topic named `/estop` not an emergency stop, even if the bridge subscribes to it? Guidance: the topic vanishes when the node or the MCU wedges; a hardware switch has to open the motor supply by itself.

## Further reading

- [REP-103](https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0103.html) is the axis and rotation convention the wheel-sign row is checking.
- [REP-105](https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0105.html) is the frame-name list both the simulator and the bridge have to share.
- [ros_gz_bridge README](https://github.com/gazebosim/ros_gz/blob/ros2/ros_gz_bridge/README.md) is the one-way `/clock` form you must not copy onto the real robot.
- [Jazzy parameters](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/How-To-Guides/Using-ros2-param.html) shows where `use_sim_time` is set, which is the line to delete on the robot launch.
