Estimated time: about 55 minutes for the software tour on a machine that can open RViz, or about 40 minutes if you only have a terminal and you stop after recording why the GUI launch did not run.

## Learning objectives

You can name the five blocks between a map goal and a velocity, point at `geometry_msgs/msg/Twist` as the only thing Nav2 owes your motor driver, run the Jazzy TurtleBot3 simulation as a tour with an initial pose, and say why that tour does not mean the Capstone navigates tonight. You can also match an empty costmap to missing odometry, a bad laser frame, a reliability mismatch, a wrong footprint, or a stray `linear.y`.

## Prerequisites

Lesson 11-01's frame story is assumed: `map`, `odom`, and `base_link` are different promises. You should know that the Capstone firmware bridge from the earlier chapter subscribes to `cmd_vel` and writes PWM. You do not need a lidar on the desk for this tour, and you should not pause the tour to buy one.

## Why this lesson sits on the path

The path is the Capstone base, then ROS 2 Jazzy, then Gazebo, then autonomy. Autonomy here means a stack that accepts a pose on a map and publishes a velocity. That stack is Nav2. It does not replace your `cmd_vel` bridge and it does not know which pin is the left motor. Watch it succeed on the TurtleBot3 the Nav2 project already wired up in simulation. Leave a parameter file for your own robot until a map, a scan, and odometry are real.

## Concepts

Nav2 is not one node. On the Jazzy getting-started path it is a behavior-tree navigator, a planner, a controller, costmaps, and recoveries, talking to each other through actions and topics.

![Nav2: a goal enters the behavior-tree navigator, a planner and a controller consult costmaps, and a Twist leaves toward the base]({{ site.imgurl }}/generated/ch11_nav2.png)

The behavior-tree navigator is the receptionist. A goal pose arrives from RViz. The tree decides whether to compute a path, follow it, or run a recovery. The next lesson opens that tree. Today you only need to know that the navigator is why the robot does not freeze forever at the first blocked cell.

The planner writes a path on the global costmap. In current Nav2 builds that is often a Smac planner, or the older NavFn grid search, chosen as a plugin. The controller tracks the path on the local costmap. Jazzy configs name DWB, which scores short rolled-out trajectories, and MPPI, which samples many velocity commands. Both publish velocity. Neither publishes PWM.

Costmaps are grids of how bad it is to stand in a cell. They need a map and a sensor stream whose quality of service connects. Laser scans are typically best effort. A subscription that insists on reliable delivery will not connect to a best-effort publisher, so the grid stays blank while Gazebo shows beams. The [Jazzy QoS article](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/Concepts/Intermediate/About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.html) states the rule: a reliable subscription matches a reliable publisher only.

The message that leaves the controller is a `geometry_msgs/msg/Twist`. In components,

$$
v_x,\; v_y,\; v_z,\; \omega_x,\; \omega_y,\; \omega_z.
$$

A differential-drive Capstone uses $$v_x$$ and $$\omega_z$$. The sideways component $$v_y$$ stays at zero because the robot cannot strafe. If a holonomic critic or a copied mecanum parameter file asks for $$v_y$$, your bridge has no honest PWM for that axis. Configure the controller for a non-holonomic base, and treat a nonzero `linear.y` as a bug in the command, not as a missing motor.

If the controller fails, the tree may spin, back up, or clear a costmap full of old obstacles. You may see that if the goal sits behind a tight gap. You are not asked to author recoveries today.

Footprint tells the costmap the body's size. A footprint much larger than the chassis makes doorways illegal. A laser yaw off by half a turn paints obstacles behind the robot. Missing odometry is worse: the local controller integrates a twist it cannot correct, and the robot drifts while the global plan still looks fine.

None of this knows your motor driver. Your bridge is the only translation from `cmd_vel` to PWM. Nav2 ending at Twist keeps the stack from growing a second copy of your H-bridge timing.

A lidar note, kept short: the simulation tour publishes its own scan. You do not need the LD19-class sensor from lesson 11-01 to finish this page. When you later want Nav2 on the physical base, that lesson's shopping note is the place to price a 360-degree scanner. A single-point ToF still will not fill a costmap the way a `LaserScan` does.

## Worked example

This is a tour of a working robot that is not yours. The [Jazzy quickstart](https://docs.nav2.org/jazzy/getting_started/quickstart/quickstart/) installs Nav2 and launches a TurtleBot3 in simulation. Quote it as a visit, not as a template you retune.

```bash
sudo apt install ros-jazzy-navigation2 ros-jazzy-nav2-bringup
source /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.bash
ros2 launch nav2_bringup tb3_simulation_launch.py headless:=False
```

`headless` defaults to true on that page. Left at the default, the 3D client does not start, and you lose the view you need in order to compare the Gazebo robot with the map. Set it false, as written.

You should see RViz and a Gazebo world with a small differential-drive robot. Jazzy uses modern Gazebo; the quickstart's gestures are the same. If the Nav2 panel is not already active, press Startup so the map appears.

The robot does not know where it is until you tell it. Click **2D Pose Estimate**, click the map where Gazebo shows the robot, and drag the heading. That pose lets AMCL finish the transform tree. Only then click the goal button (the quickstart labels it Navigaton2 Goal) and pick a free cell. In a second terminal:

```bash
source /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.bash
ros2 topic echo /cmd_vel
```

While the simulated robot moves, `linear.x` or `angular.z` leaves zero and `linear.y` stays 0.0. That Twist is what a bridge would turn into PWM. This TurtleBot3 already has a simulated drive. Do not point your Capstone bridge at this tour. Do not edit costmap resolution, inflation, or controller critics. If the robot reaches a nearby goal, the tour worked.

## Lab

Run the three commands in the worked example on a desktop session. Expected output is visual plus one topic sample:

- A Gazebo window and an RViz window, because `headless:=False`.
- After Startup, if it was required, a map in RViz.
- After **2D Pose Estimate**, the robot model sitting on that map with a particle cloud.
- After the goal, the robot moving in the simulation, and a `/cmd_vel` echo in this shape while it turns or drives:

```text
linear:
  x: 0.15
  y: 0.0
  z: 0.0
angular:
  x: 0.0
  y: 0.0
  z: 0.3
```

The numbers will not match exactly. `y` under `linear` should stay 0.0 for this differential-drive model. Paste one real sample from your echo into your notes.

If there is no display, or the launch errors before RViz, paste the error and still name the five blocks and the Twist on `cmd_vel`. The Capstone did not finish navigation homework. It still needs a map, a scan, odometry, a footprint, and your bridge.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No 3D world | `headless` left at its default true | Relaunch with `headless:=False` |
| Goal does nothing, transform tree incomplete | Initial pose never set | **2D Pose Estimate** before the goal |
| Global plan exists, local costmap empty | No odometry, or scan QoS is best effort while the subscription is reliable | `ros2 topic info /odom` and `/scan -v` |
| Robot plans through a wall, or refuses a wide door | Laser frame yaw is wrong, or the footprint does not match the body | URDF laser joint and footprint polygon |
| Command asks the base to slide sideways | Nonzero `linear.y` on a diff-drive robot | Controller must keep $$v_y = 0$$ |
| Sim moves, Capstone wheels stay still | Nav2 never wrote PWM | Your bridge is a separate process |

The [first-time robot setup guide](https://docs.nav2.org/jazzy/configuration_and_development/first_time_robot_setup_guide/) and its [URDF page](https://docs.nav2.org/jazzy/configuration_and_development/first_time_robot_setup_guide/urdf/setup_urdf/) are where a later week attaches your chassis. They are not a second lab tonight.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1

List the five Nav2 blocks from this lesson and write the message type that the controller publishes. State which process, yours or Nav2's, turns that message into PWM.

**Guidance.** Behavior-tree navigator, planner, controller, costmaps, recoveries. The type is `geometry_msgs/msg/Twist`. The bridge is yours.

### Exercise 2

You clicked a goal and the simulated robot never moved. You also never clicked **2D Pose Estimate**. What is incomplete in TF, and which click fixes the tour without editing YAML?

**Guidance.** AMCL cannot publish a trustworthy `map` to `odom` until it has a start. The click is the pose estimate, then the goal again.

### Exercise 3

A copied parameter file produces `linear.y` of 0.2 while `linear.x` is 0.1. Explain why the Capstone differential base cannot execute that Twist, and what you want `linear.y` to be.

**Guidance.** No sideways rollers. The honest command keeps $$v_y$$ at zero and uses yaw rate to turn.

### Exercise 4

`ros2 topic echo /scan` prints ranges, but the local costmap stays empty. The echo used a best-effort compatible subscriber and the costmap node was left on the default reliable profile. Describe the mismatch without proposing a new motor driver.

**Guidance.** Reliability is a subscription property. A reliable subscriber does not connect to a best-effort scan. Point at QoS, not at PWM.

### Exercise 5

In one paragraph, separate what the TurtleBot3 tour proved from what it did not prove about your Capstone. Mention the bridge explicitly.

**Guidance.** Proved: install line, initial pose, goal, Twist on `/cmd_vel` for a robot Nav2 already supports. Did not prove: your map, your lidar, your footprint, or your H-bridge.
