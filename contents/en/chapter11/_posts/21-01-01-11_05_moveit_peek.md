Estimated time: about 40 minutes of reading and writing. A full MoveIt demo is not part of the time, because this course's robot does not have an arm description.

## Learning objectives

You will say what a planning scene contains, and why joint limits and collision objects belong in that scene before anyone talks about a trajectory. You will explain why the Capstone's wheel joints are not MoveIt joints even if the URDF marks them continuous. You will list the URDF facts that must exist before `ros2 launch` of a MoveIt demo is a meaningful command, and you will treat `ros2 pkg list | grep moveit` as a sufficient software check. You will also write a budget warning for an imported learning arm without pretending you know today's price.

## Prerequisites

You should know a URDF joint from the Gazebo chapter: revolute, continuous, and fixed are different promises. You should know the Capstone is a differential-drive base whose wheels take velocity, via the `cmd_vel` bridge, not a goal angle. Nav2 from the previous lessons is the autonomy stack for that base. You do not need a servo, a gripper, or MoveIt installed in order to finish the written lab.

## Why this lesson sits on the path

The path is the Capstone base, then ROS 2 Jazzy, then Gazebo, then wheeled autonomy: a map, a Nav2 tour, a slow vision twist, and a behavior tree that ends in `cmd_vel`. MoveIt sits beside that path. It plans arms. It does not drive every joint. Putting the Capstone wheels into a planning group would ask for joint angles the wheels do not want. Recognize the tool, decline it for this robot, and learn what would have to change before an elective arm is worth a Saturday.

## Concepts

A planning scene holds the arm's joint state, the allowed collisions, and the obstacles the arm must not hit: a table, a camera mast, the robot's own links. The planner searches joint space for a path that stays inside those limits. A revolute joint's lower and upper limits are the angles the servo can reach. Velocity limits keep a hobby servo from being commanded faster than it can track.

![MoveIt plans a joint-space path for an arm inside a planning scene; the differential-drive wheels stay outside that group]({{ site.imgurl }}/generated/ch11_moveit.png)

The ROS interface most demos hide behind is MoveGroup. You send a goal for a named planning group, such as `arm` or `gripper`. MoveGroup checks the scene, calls a planner, and can execute the result through a trajectory controller. That execution path publishes joint trajectories or position goals for the joints in the group. It does not publish the differential-drive Twist your bridge already understands, and it should not be asked to.

A configuration of an arm group with $$n$$ revolute joints is

$$
q = (q_1, q_2, \ldots, q_n),
$$

with each coordinate boxed in by its URDF limits,

$$
q_i^{\min} \le q_i \le q_i^{\max}.
$$

A 6-DOF learning arm has $$n = 6$$ if all six joints are in the group. The Capstone's wheels are the wrong coordinates. Their useful input is a left speed and a right speed from `linear.x` and `angular.z`. A wheel joint is often `continuous`: no destination angle means "done." A wrist roll can be continuous. A drive wheel cannot. Success is base velocity, not a hub angle of 1.2 rad. Leave wheels out of every planning group.

If the arm overlaps the chassis at the home pose, every plan fails, or a plan ignores a link missing from the collision geometry. Fix the model before you swap planners. Limits wider than the servo stall the gearbox. Limits tighter than the hardware waste reach. Measure, then type the numbers.

The course robot fails the entry test for a demo, and that is a success. A demo wants revolute joints, position limits, and a config package that names the group. A base with wheel joints and a laser mount is a Nav2 robot. Skip MoveIt until an arm is bolted on and the chain is written down.

An SO-100 or other LeRobot-class arm is an import, not a cheap local grab, and this lesson will not invent a precise dong price. Once shipped, a 6-DOF learning-arm kit is often well above 5 million VND, plus a servo bus. Recheck any listing in the year you find it. There is no pressure to buy an arm. The wheeled Capstone does not get better because a box is on the way.

## Worked example

Read the MoveIt 2 home page until you can describe planning, the planning scene, and MoveGroup. Then write the lab's five sentences. This paragraph is a quality bar, not text to submit unchanged.

A planning scene is the arm plus nearby obstacles, updated as joint states change. Continuous wheel joints do not belong: they are driven by base velocity, not by a goal angle. Before a demo launch you need revolute arm joints, numeric limits, and a planning group that omits the wheels. The Capstone does not meet that description, so skip the launch. The command below is the only terminal check this lesson wants.

```bash
source /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.bash
ros2 pkg list | grep moveit
```

When MoveIt is installed, the expected output is a list of package names that contain `moveit`, for example lines mentioning `moveit_ros` and `moveit_ros_move_group`. The exact set depends on which metapackage you installed. When MoveIt is absent, the expected output is empty: the command prints nothing and returns to the prompt. Both results are acceptable. Do not hunt a demo launch that demands a robot description you do not have. A launch that dies looking for a URDF is not extra credit.

## Lab

Open [the MoveIt 2 documentation](https://moveit.picknik.ai/main/index.html) and write five sentences in your own file, `moveit-peek.md`. They must cover all three of the following, in any order you like, as long as a reader can find them:

- What a planning scene is.
- Why continuous wheel joints on the Capstone do not belong in a MoveIt planning group.
- What you would need before `ros2 launch` of a MoveIt demo is reasonable: a URDF with revolute joints and limits, plus a named group that excludes the wheels.

Then run the package-list command from the worked example. Expected output is either several lines containing `moveit`, pasted under the sentences, or a note that says the command printed nothing. Do not install MoveIt only to satisfy the grep. Do not start RViz for a panda or a fanuc demo unless you already have that description and you are curious after the sentences are done. Curiosity is optional. The five sentences are the lab.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `grep` prints nothing | MoveIt is not installed | Write "not installed" and keep the five sentences |
| A demo launch asks for a robot description and exits | No arm URDF on the Capstone | Stop. The lab did not ask for that launch |
| Every plan collides at the start pose | Arm links overlap the chassis in the URDF | Fix collision geometry before changing planners |
| Planner commands a wheel angle | Wheel joints were left in the planning group | Remove continuous wheel joints from the group |
| You are pricing an arm during the lab | The peek felt like a shopping task | It is not. Finish the sentences. Recheck money later |

No Vietnam shopping list belongs here. If you price an arm months from now, use the budget band in the concepts section, confirm it against a real listing, and add the servo bus. Do not treat Hshop's sensor pages as an arm catalog.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1

Name three things that belong in a planning scene for an arm mounted on a desk, and one thing that should stay out because it is not geometry.

**Guidance.** In: the arm links, the desk as a collision object, the chassis if the arm can hit it. Out: a PWM frequency, which is an electrical parameter, not a shape. Joint limits are constraints on $$q$$, so you may mention them as attached to the robot model rather than as a cardboard box.

### Exercise 2

A teammate marks both wheel joints `continuous` and adds them to the `arm` planning group so "the whole robot is in MoveIt." Explain the failure in terms of what the planner will try to choose.

**Guidance.** The planner will search for wheel angles. The base needs wheel speeds from `cmd_vel`. State that success for a wheel is a velocity, not a pose component.

### Exercise 3

List the minimum description you want in a URDF before any MoveIt demo launch, in the order you would check it. Include what you would do if the only joints you find are wheel joints.

**Guidance.** Chain of revolute joints, lower and upper limits on each, a group name, wheels excluded. If you find only wheel joints, you stop and stay with Nav2.

### Exercise 4

`ros2 pkg list | grep moveit` returns you to the prompt with no lines. What is the lab status, and which command are you not required to run next?

**Guidance.** The software check passed in the negative: MoveIt is absent, and that is allowed. You are not required to launch a demo or to apt-install a stack you will not use this week.

### Exercise 5

Write the short message you would send a teammate who wants to order a 6-DOF learning arm for the Capstone before the base navigates. Include the budget band and the servo bus, and do not invent a model-specific price.

**Guidance.** Say the arm is an import, often well above 5 million VND once shipped, plus a servo bus, recheck later. Say the current robot does not need it for Nav2.
